# Progress note, 11 Nov 2026: sensor-dropout ablation, v1 signs, real sensor faults

All work on the desktop GPU. Commit `8814c53` (dropout scoring scripts, queue E); `scripts/cert_v1_score.py` added after it. Names as before: `d1v2` = UTD-MHAD with
whole-frame video, `d2v` = MMAct with video as a third sensor; `sd50` / `sd25` = fused model retrained with one
sensor hidden in 50% / 25% of the training clips (rates fixed on 10 Nov, before any result).
All numbers are per held-out person (8 for UTD-MHAD, 20 for MMAct), 3 seeds pooled, mean [95% interval].

## 1. What was done, in order

1. **Queue D finished overnight** (10 Nov 19:04 to 23:47): 168 training runs (24 + 24 on UTD-MHAD, 60 + 60 on
   MMAct) and the lie tests for all four dropout models. No failed run; every stored prediction reproduced.
2. **Gap found.** The overnight lie tests cover only the test person. The certificate's thresholds are fitted on
   the validation person, so the dropout models also had to answer there.
   Solution: `scripts/cert_dropout.py` asks each dropout model about every clip and condition of both parts
   (as given, and with each sensor hidden in turn), using the same condition builder as the main scoring.
3. **Scoring script** `scripts/cert_dropout_score.py`. No existing scorer was edited; it imports the same
   per-person loop and the main certificate (REV3) and only changes who answers (rows A to D below).
4. **Smoke run** (fold 0, seed 0), then the **full run** (`scripts/run_journal_queue_e.sh`): 6.5 minutes, no
   failed step.
5. **Checks passed.**
   - Dropout models reproduce their stored logits (largest difference 0.125, limit 0.5).
   - A hidden sensor cannot influence the answer: with the lying sensor hidden, accuracy equals that on the
     normal clip with the same sensor hidden.
   - Same clips, labels and witnesses as the main scoring (witness agreement 1.0000).
   - Row A reproduces the stored main certificate numbers to 1.1e-16 on both datasets.

## 4. What this says, stated plainly
- Dropout training alone is not a defence against a lying sensor (about +5 points, lie clips still near 60-65%).
  It teaches the model to cope with a missing sensor, not a wrong one.
- The drop action needs a model that has seen missing sensors: hiding a sensor in the standard model (row B)
  is clearly worse.
- One dropout model can replace the per-sensor backup models at a small cost on lie clips (row C).
- One dropout model for everything (row D) is close to the main system: about 1 point lower on MMAct lie clips,
  about 1.5 points higher on normal clips (revision 1); no clear difference on UTD-MHAD.
- Row A stays the main system (fixed before these results). Rows B to D are reported as ablations.
- The intervals are not corrected for the number of comparisons.

## 5. Second ablation of the day: the v1 signs on the journal datasets

The v1 signs are the original test bench from September: S1 = does the answer wobble under harmless changes,
S2 = how much does the answer shift when one sensor is hidden. Queue C had computed them on 10 Nov; only the
scoring was missing.

1. **Script** `scripts/cert_v1_score.py`. No existing scorer edited: sign definitions imported from the
   conference scorer (`cert_score.py`), per-person loop as for every other number.
2. **Limit, by design:** S1 only exists for sensors with a harmless-change model (UTD-MHAD: IMU; MMAct: watch,
   phone). Skeleton and video have none. The S1 strength level is chosen on validation (level 3 in every fold).
3. **Checks passed:** signals and witness files agree on the fused answer in 100% of rows (41,328 and 455,328);
   C3's lie AUROC is identical in the two imported scorers; REV3 reproduces the stored main numbers to 1.1e-16.

### Results (revision 1)
| System | UTD-MHAD lie AUROC | naming % | right on lie clips % | MMAct lie AUROC | naming % | right on lie clips % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Confidence alone (1 - msp) | 0.726 | - | - | 0.675 | - | - |
| V1 (confidence + S1 + S2) | 0.781 | 56.1 | 64.7 | 0.722 | 47.9 | 57.6 |
| C3 (confidence + witnesses) | 0.871 | 82.7 | 74.0 | 0.850 | 84.7 | 75.0 |
| REV3 (main) | 0.901 | 82.2 | 75.7 | 0.930 | 86.0 | 75.5 |
| REV3 + V1 signs | 0.900 | 82.2 | 75.6 | 0.930 | 86.0 | 75.6 |

- **Each v1 sign alone is weaker than plain confidence** at catching lies (S1 0.574 / 0.599, S2 0.663 / 0.630).
- **Combined, V1 beats confidence a little:** +0.054 [+0.040, +0.068] (8/8) and +0.047 [+0.039, +0.055] (20/20).
- **REV3 is far ahead of V1:** lie AUROC +0.120 (8/8) and +0.208 (20/20); naming +26.1 and +38.1 points;
  right on lie clips +10.9 and +17.9 points. V1 keeps 2 to 4 more points on normal clips, because it rarely
  drops a sensor.
- **Adding the v1 signs to REV3 changes nothing for lies** (lie AUROC difference at most 0.001; naming identical).
- **One small exception, natural mistakes on MMAct:** REV3 + V1 spots the model's own mistakes slightly better
  than REV3 (error AUROC 0.849 vs 0.829; +0.020 [+0.007, +0.033], 17/20). On UTD-MHAD the difference goes the
  other way and is not clear. Plain confidence is still as good or better for natural mistakes on both datasets
  (0.926 and 0.859).
- Conclusion: the witnesses and MI carry the certificate; the v1 test bench is reported as an ablation row and
  is not needed in the main system. It also needs many extra forward passes per clip (8 draws x 3 levels).

## 6. Third experiment of the day: real sensor faults (frozen transfer test)

**Question.** The certificate was built and tuned on lies (one sensor replaced by a recording of a different
activity). Does it, unchanged, also catch the everyday faults real sensors have?
Plan fixed in advance in `docs/plan_faults_2026-11-11.md` (approved before any result).

**Faults** (three strengths each, fixed in the plan; `src/trustmi/faults.py`):
- **Drift:** the reading slowly creeps off over the clip (accelerometer up to 0.05 / 0.2 / 0.5 g, gyroscope
  up to 1 / 5 / 20 deg/s, orientation up to 2 / 10 / 30 deg).
- **Gap:** 10 / 25 / 50% of the clip is lost and the sensor repeats its last value (for video: frozen frames).
- **Timing error:** one sensor runs 0.2 / 0.5 / 1.0 s out of step (MMAct only, as fixed in the plan). Drift is applied to IMU, watch and phone; gap to every sensor.

**No-cheating rule.** Every threshold, naming rule and policy is fitted on the validation person using the lie
test only, then applied to the fault clips of the test person without any change. One extra row, clearly labelled
"REV3 known faults", was allowed to see fault clips while fitting; it shows the cost of never having seen them.

### What was done, in order
1. **Fault code and tests:** 18 unit tests (exact drift ramp, units, clipping to the training range, gap share,
   exact timing shift, same draw every run). Three bugs planted on purpose were each caught by the tests.
2. **Strength check on real data** (`scripts/check_faults.py`, fold 0): no drifted value had to be clipped; gap shares
   and timing shifts exact.
3. **Broken videos** (`scripts/extract_video_faults.py`): every video decoded once and run through the same
   frozen video network, clean and with frozen frames or a time shift; one draw per clip, shared by all camera
   views. UTD-MHAD 861 videos in 45 s; MMAct about 34,000 videos in about 2 h.
   Check: recomputed clean features match the stored ones (largest difference 0.05 and 0.02 of a feature's normal
   spread); the broken versions differ far more (mean 0.07 to 0.64, up to 12).
4. **Problem hit, solved:** the stored UTD-MHAD clip names could not be read (Python refuses this file type by
   default); fixed by opening the file in the allowed mode and checking the clip order before extraction.
5. **Queue F** (`scripts/run_journal_queue_f.sh`, 12:00 to 12:24): every model answered every clip under every
   fault: main model, witnesses, MI, TMC, ECML, dropout model. No failed step; all models reproduce their
   stored clean answers.
6. **Scoring** (`scripts/cert_fault_score.py`). Checks passed: clean clips identical between the lie and fault
   files (difference 0); each frozen fit reproduces the stored lie numbers exactly.
7. **Earlier comparison check fixed:** `check_identical.py` had paired the TMC and ECML rows of the same clip
   wrongly; fixed (`--by method`). After the fix, all 11 lie-result files are identical to before.

### Results (revision 1 unless stated; mean over test people; 3 seeds pooled)

**Harm.** Most faults barely change the answer. Drift and timing errors flip at most 6% of answers, even at
the strongest level. Only a big gap on the strongest sensor really hurts:

| Worst case | Accuracy, clean -> broken |
|---|---|
| UTD-MHAD, IMU 50% lost | 94.7 -> 67.6 |
| UTD-MHAD, skeleton 50% lost | 94.7 -> 89.5 |
| MMAct, video 50% frozen | 89.1 -> 71.0 |
| MMAct, watch 50% lost | 89.1 -> 86.2 |

**Catching harmful faults** (AUROC, fault clips whose answer went wrong vs clean clips):

| System | UTD-MHAD | MMAct |
|---|---|---|
| Confidence alone (1 - msp) | **0.926** | **0.898** |
| C3 | 0.876 | 0.794 |
| REV3 (main) | 0.838 | 0.774 |
| TMC alone | 0.876 | 0.860 |
| ECML alone | 0.842 | 0.829 |
| D (dropout model) | 0.850 | 0.781 |
| REV3 known faults (labelled) | 0.852 | 0.751 |

- REV3 minus confidence: UTD-MHAD -0.088 [-0.127, -0.048] (0/8 people favour REV3);
  MMAct -0.124 [-0.140, -0.108] (0/20). Confidence also wins at the strongest gaps.
- On **all** fault clips (harmful or not) REV3 reacts slightly more than confidence: MMAct +0.026
  [+0.022, +0.030] (20/20); UTD-MHAD +0.011 [-0.002, +0.023] (not clear). The certificate sees that a sensor is
  off, but cannot tell whether that matters for the answer.

**Naming the faulty sensor** (harmful fault clips; chance 33%):

| System | UTD-MHAD | MMAct |
|---|---|---|
| C3 | 49.9 | 46.1 |
| REV3 (main) | 50.5 | 47.4 |
| TMC alone | 27.2 | 17.6 |
| ECML alone | 42.7 | 14.0 |
| REV3 known faults (labelled) | 51.8 | 47.5 |

- REV3 minus TMC: +23.3 [+14.8, +31.9] (8/8) and +29.8 [+27.2, +32.3] (20/20).
- REV3 minus ECML: +7.8 (not clear) and +33.4 [+31.0, +35.8] (20/20).
- Naming is best at big gaps (57% on both datasets) and weakest for small drift and timing errors.

**Acting on it** (use / drop policy; accuracy on fault clips):

| System | UTD-MHAD: do nothing -> rev 1 / rev 2 | MMAct: do nothing -> rev 1 / rev 2 |
|---|---|---|
| REV3 (main) | 90.7 -> 87.5 / 89.0 | 87.6 -> 86.0 / 87.0 |
| C3 | 90.7 -> 86.8 / 89.0 | 87.6 -> 86.4 / 87.1 |
| TMC alone | 92.0 -> 92.0 / 92.0 | 87.8 -> 87.8 / 87.8 |

- No system's policy improves on doing nothing for faults. Two reasons: most fault clips were not harmed, so a
  flag on them is a false alarm; and a half-broken sensor still carries information, so dropping it rarely helps
  (dropout model on UTD-MHAD, IMU 50% lost: kept 72.7%, hidden 68.5%).
- Answering only the 80% most trusted clips (clean and fault clips together): confidence 97.5 (UTD-MHAD) and
  94.4 (MMAct); REV3 92.5 / 89.3 (revision 1), 94.9 / 92.1 (revision 2, post hoc, labelled).

**Seeing faults beforehand does not fix it.** "REV3 known faults" is about 0.014 better than REV3 at catching
harmful faults on UTD-MHAD (not clear) and 0.023 worse on MMAct ([+0.008, +0.039] in favour of the frozen REV3,
14/20). The gap comes from what the certificate measures, not from lack of practice.

### What this says, stated plainly
- The certificate is built for **confident lies**, where the model is sure but wrong. On that test, confidence is
  weak (lie AUROC 0.726 / 0.675) and REV3 is strong (0.901 / 0.930), see section 5.
- Ordinary faults behave the opposite way: when they break the answer, the model becomes unsure, and plain
  confidence catches that better than any system tested, including TMC and ECML.
- The certificate still **names the faulty sensor** clearly better than chance and far better than TMC and ECML.
- The use / drop policy should not be applied to faults; it costs accuracy.
- This is reported as a frozen result and a stated limitation. A combined rule (abstain on low confidence, use
  the certificate for lies and naming) would be designed after seeing these numbers, so it can only be added as
  a labelled post hoc revision, tested on both the lie and fault tests. To be decided with the supervisor.
- The intervals are not corrected for the number of comparisons.
