# Progress note, 11–12 Nov 2026: WearGait-PD as the third dataset

Status at 09:15 on 12 Nov: data, folds, training code and all test benches built and tested; training in its
last stage (MI matchers); full bench run and scoring still to come. **No bench result yet.** Everything below
is what was done, in order, with what each step showed. Exact settings: `docs/methods_notes.md`, section C.

## 1. Why a third dataset
The two datasets so far have few people (UTD-MHAD 8, MMAct 20), so per-person intervals are wide, and MMAct
gave only one real missing sensor (247 clips from 6 people). WearGait-PD has 185 people (100 with Parkinson's
disease, 85 controls), 13 body-worn sensors and real sensor dropouts.

## 2. Audit (11 Nov; `docs/data_audit_weargait.md`, `scripts/audit_weargait.py`)
- Read all 1,473 recordings (8 lab tasks per person, 100 Hz), without training anything.
- **Found:** 13 synchronised IMUs in 97% of recordings; a per-sample activity label (walk, turn, standing,
  sitting, turn-to-sit, tandem walk, six balance stances); real partial dropouts (a sensor losing about a
  quarter of its samples) in about 11% of recordings; no recording with a timing glitch.
- **Expected before the audit, and wrong:** that the dataset was gait-only and unsuitable. The labels make it a
  real activity-recognition dataset.
- **Concerns recorded:** fewer and easier classes than the other datasets; each task contains only some
  activities, so clips must be cut from labelled segments; short activities (turns about 2 s).

## 3. Plan (11 Nov; `docs/plan_weargait_2026-11-11.md`, approved before anything was built)
Six choices fixed in advance: 6 classes (balance stances merged into standing; getting up from a chair dropped,
too few long segments); 2 s clips with a 1 s step, at most 8 per segment; 13 IMUs grouped into 3 sensors (arms,
trunk, legs); accelerometer and gyroscope only; 10 person-wise folds, each with 10 Parkinson's people; clips with
real dropouts kept out of training and used as a separate test.

## 4. Building the clips and folds (11 Nov)
- **Build script** (`scripts/build_weargait.py`): anti-aliasing filter, 100 Hz to 50 Hz, clips inside one
  activity, clean vs real-dropout clips. Tested first on synthetic recordings with known answers (5 tests);
  three planted bugs were each caught.
- **Problem hit, solved:** one test of my own placed a gap where no clip could reach it (the 8-clip cap stops
  9 s into a segment); the test, not the code, was wrong, and was corrected.
- **Result** (`logs/build_weargait.log`): 36,318 clips, 32,735 clean and 3,583 real-dropout. Class shares as
  planned: standing 38.8%, walk 37.9%, tandem walk 7.8%, turn 7.6%, sitting 5.9%, turn-to-sit 2.2%.
- **Folds** (`splits/weargait_v1.json`): every fold has all 6 activities and 10 Parkinson's people; every
  person is tested exactly once. All 14 data tests passed on the real data.
- **Found:** 4 people (2 Parkinson's, 2 controls) have no clean clip at all, only real-dropout clips; and the
  real-dropout clips are very unevenly spread (12 in fold 7, 678 in fold 6). Both recorded before training.

## 5. Plugging the dataset into the model code (11 Nov)
- New entries only (dataset "d3", three sensor encoders, model names); nothing existing changed. Checked: lie
  results of the other datasets bit-identical (13 of 13 comparisons), backup file unchanged.
- **Problem found and solved before it could matter:** neighbouring clips of one activity overlap by 1 s, so a
  "wrong partner" for the MI training or a donor for a swap could share half its signal with the real one.
  Rule added: never from the same labelled segment. Checked on 3,200 draws: none violated it.
- Smoke run: every model learns; one full run of the main model took 91 s.

## 6. Training (11 Nov 19:15 to 12 Nov about 09:50; `logs/journal_queue_h.log`)
About 340 runs: 3 witnesses, main model, 3 backups, shuffled-label control, dropout model, TMC, ECML, two MI
matchers, each for 10 folds x 3 seeds. No run failed. Accuracy, mean over folds and seeds:

| Model | Accuracy | Balanced (macro-F1) |
|---|---|---|
| Main model (arms + trunk + legs) | 96.5% | 0.935 |
| Legs alone | 96.1% | 0.918 |
| Trunk alone | 91.7% | 0.861 |
| Arms alone | 88.5% | 0.804 |
| Without the legs | 92.9% | 0.880 |
| Dropout-trained model | 96.9% | 0.938 |
| TMC / ECML | 96.6% / 96.4% | 0.933 / 0.925 |
| Shuffled-label control | 49.2% | 0.196 (chance 0.167) |

- The control learned nothing, as it should: the real models do not exploit an accident of the data.
- The legs carry almost all of the information; the task is easy, as the audit warned.
- **Side issue:** the GPU ran at only 40–50%, because the models and clips are tiny and Python, not the GPU,
  sets the pace. Not changed (batch size is fixed by the plan); instead the MI matchers ran in a second window.
- **Record-keeping:** 16 runs (2 ECML, 14 MI) started while new bench code was on disk but not yet committed and
  were marked "uncommitted". **Checked:** all 16 were retrained from the committed code and compared with the
  originals (`scripts/compare_reruns.py`, `logs/compare_reruns.log`): weights, training curves, predictions and
  scores are byte-for-byte identical in 16 of 16. A second sign of determinism: one MI run that both training
  windows happened to train at the same time came out identical too.
- **Problem hit, solved:** the first attempt at this check passed all 16 run names as one long name, because
  the terminal shell (zsh) does not split a text variable into words as bash does; nothing was copied or
  changed. The names were stored as a list instead, with count checks (16 runs, 32 backed-up items) before
  retraining.

## 7. Test benches (12 Nov; queue I)
- **Operators for sensor groups:** each IMU of a group gets its own random mounting rotation; donors never come
  from an overlapping clip. **Faults for groups:** drift, gap, timing error on the whole group, every IMU drifting
  in its own direction. **Real-dropout test:** built like the MMAct no-watch test; its main result was named in
  the code and the plan before any result: the main certificate's use / drop accuracy on clips where exactly one
  group lost data, against doing nothing and against the backup for that group.
- **Unit of analysis:** the scorers assumed one test person per fold; for WearGait-PD the fold (about 18 people)
  is the unit, with intervals over 10 folds. For the other datasets the scorers give exactly what they gave before.
- **Fix in an old script:** `cert_signals.py` would have crashed on any journal dataset now that backups are
  chosen; it now does what its own comment said. Checked: the stored MMAct and UTD-MHAD signal files indeed have
  no backup columns, so nothing in them changes.
- **Checks:** 6 bench tests, including a byte-for-byte comparison of old and new operator code on MMAct-shaped
  data; three planted bugs caught. Old lie results identical; the MMAct certificate still reproduces the paper's
  numbers (difference 1.1e-16).
- **Problem hit, solved:** the first test run on the real data failed 4 tests. The loader recorded the fold's
  list of training people from the split file, but the 4 people without clean clips contribute nothing; the
  operators' safety check saw the mismatch and stopped. Fixed by recording the people the statistics really
  come from; the statistics themselves did not change. Then 92 of 92 tests passed.
- **Backups** chosen by the fixed rule: drop arms or trunk -> legs witness; drop legs -> arms + trunk model.

## 8. Smoke run of the benches (fold 0, seed 0; a code check, not results)
- Every step ran and every reproduction check passed; same-activity control swaps possible for 99.7% of clips.
- First impressions only: a lie in the legs changes 78% of answers, in the trunk 9%, in the arms 6%. Lies are
  very visible here (witnesses disagree on 96–98% of lie clips vs 14% of normal ones; rough lie AUROC of the frozen MI signal 0.979).
  Everyday faults barely hurt (at most about 1 point in the dropout model's quick summary).
- The old warning signs took 14 min for one fold and seed, so about 7 h for the full run; they serve one
  ablation row only and run last.
