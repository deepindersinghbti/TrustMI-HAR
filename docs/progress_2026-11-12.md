# Progress note, 11–12 Nov 2026: WearGait-PD as the third dataset, then the follow-up experiments

Status at 17:30 on 12 Nov: WearGait-PD data, folds, training, all test benches and all scoring done (sections
1–9); the follow-up experiments of 12 Nov planned, run and scored (sections 10–12). Exact settings:
`docs/methods_notes.md`, sections C, D and E.

## 1. Why a third dataset
The two datasets so far have few people (UTD-MHAD 8, MMAct 20), so per-person intervals are wide, and MMAct
gave only one real missing sensor (247 clips from 6 people). WearGait-PD has 185 people (100 with Parkinson's
disease, 85 controls), 13 body-worn sensors and real sensor dropouts.

## 2. Audit (11 Nov; `docs/data_audit_weargait.md`, `scripts/audit_weargait.py`)
- Read all 1,473 recordings (8 lab tasks per person, 100 Hz), without training anything.
- **Found:** 13 synchronised IMUs in 97% of recordings; a per-sample activity label (walk, turn, standing,
  sitting, turn-to-sit, tandem walk, six balance stances); real partial dropouts (a sensor losing about a
  quarter of its samples) in about 11% of recordings; no recording with a timing glitch.
- **Expected before the audit, and wrong:** that the dataset was gait-only and unsuitable. The labels make it a
  real activity-recognition dataset.
- **Concerns recorded:** fewer and easier classes than the other datasets; each task contains only some
  activities, so clips must be cut from labelled segments; short activities (turns about 2 s).

## 3. Plan (11 Nov; `docs/plan_weargait_2026-11-11.md`, approved before anything was built)
Six choices fixed in advance: 6 classes (balance stances merged into standing; getting up from a chair dropped,
too few long segments); 2 s clips with a 1 s step, at most 8 per segment; 13 IMUs grouped into 3 sensors (arms,
trunk, legs); accelerometer and gyroscope only; 10 person-wise folds, each with 10 Parkinson's people; clips with
real dropouts kept out of training and used as a separate test.

## 4. Building the clips and folds (11 Nov)
- **Build script** (`scripts/build_weargait.py`): anti-aliasing filter, 100 Hz to 50 Hz, clips inside one
  activity, clean vs real-dropout clips. Tested first on synthetic recordings with known answers (5 tests);
  three planted bugs were each caught.
- **Problem hit, solved:** one test of my own placed a gap where no clip could reach it (the 8-clip cap stops
  9 s into a segment); the test, not the code, was wrong, and was corrected.
- **Result** (`logs/build_weargait.log`): 36,318 clips, 32,735 clean and 3,583 real-dropout. Class shares as
  planned: standing 38.8%, walk 37.9%, tandem walk 7.8%, turn 7.6%, sitting 5.9%, turn-to-sit 2.2%.
- **Folds** (`splits/weargait_v1.json`): every fold has all 6 activities and 10 Parkinson's people; every
  person is tested exactly once. All 14 data tests passed on the real data.
- **Found:** 4 people (2 Parkinson's, 2 controls) have no clean clip at all, only real-dropout clips; and the
  real-dropout clips are very unevenly spread (12 in fold 7, 678 in fold 6). Both recorded before training.

## 5. Plugging the dataset into the model code (11 Nov)
- New entries only (dataset "d3", three sensor encoders, model names); nothing existing changed. Checked: lie
  results of the other datasets bit-identical (13 of 13 comparisons), backup file unchanged.
- **Problem found and solved before it could matter:** neighbouring clips of one activity overlap by 1 s, so a
  "wrong partner" for the MI training or a donor for a swap could share half its signal with the real one.
  Rule added: never from the same labelled segment. Checked on 3,200 draws: none violated it.
- Smoke run: every model learns; one full run of the main model took 91 s.

## 6. Training (11 Nov 19:15 to 12 Nov about 09:50; `logs/journal_queue_h.log`)
About 340 runs: 3 witnesses, main model, 3 backups, shuffled-label control, dropout model, TMC, ECML, two MI
matchers, each for 10 folds x 3 seeds. No run failed. Accuracy, mean over folds and seeds:

| Model | Accuracy | Balanced (macro-F1) |
|---|---|---|
| Main model (arms + trunk + legs) | 96.5% | 0.935 |
| Legs alone | 96.1% | 0.918 |
| Trunk alone | 91.7% | 0.861 |
| Arms alone | 88.5% | 0.804 |
| Without the legs | 92.9% | 0.880 |
| Dropout-trained model | 96.9% | 0.938 |
| TMC / ECML | 96.6% / 96.4% | 0.933 / 0.925 |
| Shuffled-label control | 49.2% | 0.196 (chance 0.167) |

- The control learned nothing, as it should: the real models do not exploit an accident of the data.
- The legs carry almost all of the information; the task is easy, as the audit warned.
- **Side issue:** the GPU ran at only 40–50%, because the models and clips are tiny and Python, not the GPU,
  sets the pace. Not changed (batch size is fixed by the plan); instead the MI matchers ran in a second window.
- **Record-keeping:** 16 runs (2 ECML, 14 MI) started while new bench code was on disk but not yet committed and
  were marked "uncommitted". **Checked:** all 16 were retrained from the committed code and compared with the
  originals (`scripts/compare_reruns.py`, `logs/compare_reruns.log`): weights, training curves, predictions and
  scores are byte-for-byte identical in 16 of 16. A second sign of determinism: one MI run that both training
  windows happened to train at the same time came out identical too.
- **Problem hit, solved:** the first attempt at this check passed all 16 run names as one long name, because
  the terminal shell (zsh) does not split a text variable into words as bash does; nothing was copied or
  changed. The names were stored as a list instead, with count checks (16 runs, 32 backed-up items) before
  retraining.

## 7. Test benches (12 Nov; queue I)
- **Operators for sensor groups:** each IMU of a group gets its own random mounting rotation; donors never come
  from an overlapping clip. **Faults for groups:** drift, gap, timing error on the whole group, every IMU drifting
  in its own direction. **Real-dropout test:** built like the MMAct no-watch test; its main result was named in
  the code and the plan before any result: the main certificate's use / drop accuracy on clips where exactly one
  group lost data, against doing nothing and against the backup for that group.
- **Unit of analysis:** the scorers assumed one test person per fold; for WearGait-PD the fold (about 18 people)
  is the unit, with intervals over 10 folds. For the other datasets the scorers give exactly what they gave before.
- **Fix in an old script:** `cert_signals.py` would have crashed on any journal dataset now that backups are
  chosen; it now does what its own comment said. Checked: the stored MMAct and UTD-MHAD signal files indeed have
  no backup columns, so nothing in them changes.
- **Checks:** 6 bench tests, including a byte-for-byte comparison of old and new operator code on MMAct-shaped
  data; three planted bugs caught. Old lie results identical; the MMAct certificate still reproduces the paper's
  numbers (difference 1.1e-16).
- **Problem hit, solved:** the first test run on the real data failed 4 tests. The loader recorded the fold's
  list of training people from the split file, but the 4 people without clean clips contribute nothing; the
  operators' safety check saw the mismatch and stopped. Fixed by recording the people the statistics really
  come from; the statistics themselves did not change. Then 92 of 92 tests passed.
- **Backups** chosen by the fixed rule: drop arms or trunk -> legs witness; drop legs -> arms + trunk model.

## 8. Smoke run of the benches (fold 0, seed 0; a code check, not results)
- Every step ran and every reproduction check passed; same-activity control swaps possible for 99.7% of clips.
- First impressions only: a lie in the legs changes 78% of answers, in the trunk 9%, in the arms 6%. Lies are
  very visible here (witnesses disagree on 96–98% of lie clips vs 14% of normal ones; rough lie AUROC of the frozen MI signal 0.979).
  Everyday faults barely hurt (at most about 1 point in the dropout model's quick summary).
- The old warning signs took 14 min for one fold and seed, so about 7 h for the full run; they serve one
  ablation row only and run last.

## 9. Results (full run: 10 folds x 3 seeds; queue I 11:21–14:48, scoring 15:15–15:30)
All numbers are means over the 10 folds unless marked "pooled"; intervals are 95% over folds. Logs:
`logs/journal_queue_i.log`, `logs/score_{mi,fault,realdrop,dropout}_d3.log`. No run failed.

### 9.1 Lies (`logs/score_mi_d3.log`)
| System (on the main model, revision 1) | Lie AUROC | Names the liar | Accuracy on lie clips after use / drop | Normal clips |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **REV3 (main)** | **0.980** [0.977, 0.983] | **97.7%** | **93.5%** | 96.4% |
| C3 (conference certificate) | 0.965 | 94.4% | 92.0% | 96.2% |
| TMC alone | 0.947 | 89.4% | 88.6% | 96.3% |
| ECML alone | 0.930 | 87.9% | 88.5% | 96.2% |

- REV3 beats C3, TMC and ECML on lie detection, naming and accuracy after use / drop in **10 of 10 folds**.
  Same picture with the certificate placed on TMC or on ECML (REV3 0.980 / 0.981).
- A lie in the legs is the dangerous one: the fused model alone is right on only 10.7% of those clips, while its
  confidence stays high (0.96 vs 0.99 on clean clips, from the warning-signs run), so confidence cannot flag it.
  REV3 names the legs 97.4% of the time and the backup restores the answer.
- One small loss, as before: for ordinary mistakes (not lies) C3 is slightly better (error AUROC 0.931 vs 0.922,
  REV3 worse in 10 of 10 folds).
- Dataset MI between body groups is the highest of the three datasets (own matcher 1.7–2.1 nats) and varies
  little between folds.

### 9.2 Faults (`logs/score_fault_d3.log`; frozen transfer from the lie bench)
- Harm is small: worst case (largest timing shift on the legs) changes 4.4% of answers, accuracy 96.6% -> 93.9%.
- Catching harmful faults: confidence 0.947, TMC 0.931, dropout model 0.880, **REV3 0.862**, ECML 0.856, C3 0.689.
  Confidence beats REV3 in 10 of 10 folds (-0.085); REV3 beats C3 in 10 of 10 (+0.173).
- Naming the faulty group (harmful clips): REV3 53.6% (chance 33%), better than C3 (43.0%) and TMC (44.0%) in
  10 of 10 folds, but level with ECML (54.7%). Weaker than on the other two datasets.
- Use / drop does not help on faults (fault-clip accuracy 96.2% -> 95.9%); "known faults" is worse, not better.
- Same conclusion as on UTD-MHAD and MMAct.

### 9.3 Dropout-model comparison (`logs/score_dropout_d3.log`)
- Dropout model as backup or everywhere: same as separate backups (lie-clip accuracy -0.2 points, 4–5 of 10 folds
  better). Hiding the lying group in a model never trained to work without it: -6.0 points, 0 of 10 folds better.

### 9.4 Real dropouts: main result named in advance (`logs/score_realdrop_d3.log`)
- **Problem hit, solved:** the scorer crashed after the first table (`rd.clip` reached pandas' built-in `clip`
  function instead of the column of clip IDs). Changed to `rd['clip']` in 4 places, which only count and print;
  no number depends on them. Re-run: completed.
- **Main result** (REV3, revision 1, clips with exactly one group missing; 3,250 clips x 3 seeds):

| | Pooled | Mean over folds |
|---|---|---|
| Doing nothing | 94.3% | 94.9% |
| **REV3 use / drop** | **95.2%** | **95.6%** |
| Backup for the known missing group | 92.9% | – |

  Paired over folds: +0.7 points [-2.4, +3.8]; REV3 better in 2 folds, level in 1, worse in 7 (by 0.1–1.8 points).
  **Not a general improvement.** The whole gain comes from fold 2 (85.3% -> 98.0%); without it the effect is
  zero or slightly negative.
- Spotting dropout clips at all is near chance for every system (AUROC REV3 0.591, dropout model 0.591,
  confidence 0.536). TMC and ECML alone are slightly more accurate on these clips (about 96%), as they are on clean ones.
- Real vs simulated: simulated removal of the whole legs group drops the fused model to 52.5%; real leg dropouts
  only to 92.7%. REV3 flags 8–20% of real dropout clips but 66–67% of simulated ones.

### 9.5 Why: what the real dropouts are (post hoc look, descriptive only; nothing was refitted)
Done after the main result, to explain it; it changes no number above and is labelled as post hoc.
- **No real-dropout clip loses its whole group.** Of the 3,250 single-group clips: arms lose 1 of 2 wrist IMUs;
  trunk 1 of 3 (or 2 in 7 clips); legs usually 1 or 2 of 8 IMUs. The affected IMU is missing for much of the clip
  (on average 44% of the samples for arms, 79% for trunk, 63% for legs), but the other IMUs of the group keep
  working, so the fused model still sees most of the group.
- Legs, by number of IMUs lost (pooled): 1 IMU (1,090 clips): nothing 96.3% vs REV3 95.7%; 2 IMUs (479):
  96.7% vs 94.6%; 6 IMUs (126): **73.0% vs 84.7%**; 7 IMUs (160, one person): **68.5% vs 96.9%**.
- **Fold 2 is one person:** a control (WHC003) whose recordings all lack 7 of the 8 leg IMUs. Without this
  person, fold 2 is 98.8% for both. The four other people where REV3 helps most (folds 3, 4, 5: +6 to +20
  points) are the ones whose clips lose 6 of the 8 leg IMUs; the people where it hurts most (-3 to -7 points)
  lose only 1 IMU of a group, so REV3 drops a group that was still mostly working.
- **Reading:** REV3 helps when a group is almost gone, which is the case it was built for, and costs a little when
  only a small part of a group is missing. The pre-registered main result is therefore near zero on this dataset
  because almost all real dropouts here are partial.

### 9.6 Summary for the supervisor
1. Lies: REV3 is the best certificate on all 10 folds of a third, much larger dataset.
2. Faults: confidence remains the better alarm; same finding on all three datasets.
3. Dropout model: no better than separate backups; hiding without retraining is clearly worse.
4. Real dropouts: the named main result is a near tie (+0.7 points, 2 of 10 folds better). Post hoc, the reason is
   clear: real dropouts here remove part of a group, not all of it; REV3 helps a lot when most of a group is gone.

## 11. Planned test: IMUs switched off one by one ("IMU-off test")
### 11.1 What was done
- **Bench** (`scripts/cert_conditions.py`, `build_imuoff_conditions`): on the clean test clips of every fold, k IMUs
  of one group set to the training mean for the whole clip; arms k = 1–2, trunk 1–3, legs 1–8 (13 conditions plus
  clean). Each clip has its own random order of IMUs, so k + 1 is always k plus one more IMU.
- **Code:** a fifth condition set `--conditions imuoff` in the four model-running scripts; nothing else changed.
- **Tests** (`tests/test_imuoff.py`, 11 tests): exactly k IMUs off and everything else byte-identical; nested;
  deterministic; every IMU switched off equally often; "all off" equals `r_fill` on the real data. Four planted
  bugs (off by one, order keyed on row position, wrong channel mapping, -0.0 instead of 0) were each caught.
- **Nothing old changed** (`scripts/check_unchanged_imuoff.sh`, `logs/check_unchanged_imuoff.log`): lie, fault and
  real-dropout benches of every dataset re-run for fold 0 seed 0 and compared row by row: all identical.
- **Runs:** smoke run (fold 0, seed 0) as a code check, then queue J (`scripts/run_journal_queue_j.sh`,
  17:01–17:10, `logs/journal_queue_j.log`); no step failed. Scorer: `scripts/cert_imuoff_score.py`
  (`logs/score_imuoff_d3.log`).
- **Problem hit, solved:** the scorer's summary line said "8 IMU-off conditions" instead of 13 (it counted values
  of k, not group and k pairs). Display only; fixed before the full run.
- **Checks passed:** clean rows identical to the lie bench; every frozen fit reproduces the stored lie numbers;
  "all IMUs off" equals the stored `r_fill` rows exactly (largest difference 0, all four model files); every system
  scored on the same 1,374,870 rows.

| 3 | 92.6 | 92.8 | +0.14 [-0.14, +0.42] | 6 / 10 |

| 6 | 75.4 | 87.8 | +12.37 [+10.49, +14.25] | 10 / 10 |

| 8 (= `r_fill`, known before) | 52.5 | 92.0 | +39.52 [+36.47, +42.57] | 10 / 10 |

Predictions (plan A.6), all five **confirmed**:
- P1 (1–2 leg IMUs off: loss between 0 and 2 points): -0.28 and -0.14. Confirmed.
- P2 (7 off: clear gain): +23.73, interval above 0. Confirmed.
- P3 (gain grows with k): slope positive in 10 of 10 folds, +5.22 [+4.80, +5.63] points per IMU. Confirmed.
- P4 (crossover between 3 and 6): k* = 3. Confirmed.
- P5 (arms, trunk within +/- 1.5 points): -0.76 to +0.76. Confirmed.

Supporting, stated plainly:
- At 1 leg IMU the loss is small but real (worse in 7 of 10 folds; interval just reaches 0).
- REV3 is not the best system everywhere. With 4–6 leg IMUs off, TMC alone (93.7 / 92.5 / 91.1) and the dropout
  model (92.5 / 90.7 / 89.7) beat REV3 (90.4 / 88.4 / 87.8); REV3 is level at 7 and best at 8 (92.0 vs TMC 87.0).
- Detection rises with k: REV3 AUROC 0.53 (1 leg IMU) to 0.91 (8), always above C3, TMC, ECML and confidence.
- Naming improves with k: legs named on 19% of flagged clips with 1 IMU off, 75% with 4, 96–98% with 7–8.
- Arms and trunk: REV3 loses 0.7–0.8 points when some but not all IMUs are off (the drop goes to the legs
  witness, slightly weaker than the full model) and gains 0.8 when the whole trunk is off; doing nothing is the
  best answer for partial arm or trunk removal.
- Reading: the post hoc explanation of section 9.5 holds on every person in a controlled test: REV3's drop pays off
  once most of a group is gone and costs a little when only a small part is missing. Limit: whole-clip removal,
  while the real dropouts were partial in time.

## 12. Post hoc rules (labelled extra rows; `scripts/cert_posthoc_score.py`, `logs/score_posthoc.log`)
Designed after seeing results; never the main result. Check first: the plain REV3 row computed by this script
reproduced the stored revision-1 numbers of all 9 benches exactly (largest difference 1.1e-16).
- **Rule (a), confidence as a second alarm** (alarm = larger validation percentile of REV3's score and of
  1 - msp). Faults: catching harmful faults rises in every person / fold: UTD-MHAD 0.838 -> 0.900 (confidence
  0.926), MMAct 0.774 -> 0.873 (0.898), WearGait-PD 0.862 -> 0.950 (0.947). Cost on lies: lie AUROC -0.026,
  -0.020, -0.003, worse in every unit, still far above confidence (e.g. 0.875 vs 0.726). "Answer the safest 80%"
  slightly better almost everywhere. On missing sensors (no-watch, real dropouts, IMU-off) it does not help
  detection (IMU-off: about -0.03).
- **Rule (b), drop only when sure which sensor** (naming margin >= tau, tau chosen on validation lies). The
  validation lies chose tau = 0, i.e. no change, in 2 of 8, 13 of 20 and 7 of 10 folds. Effects are tiny: real
  dropouts pooled 95.2 -> 95.4; IMU-off within 0.2 points; MMAct no-watch person 2 unchanged at 32.8% (doing
  nothing 51.3%), because those wrong drops were made with a confident name.
- Reading: (a) is a cheap fix for ordinary faults at a small cost on lies; (b), fitted on lies, does not transfer to
  missing sensors. Both stay labelled extra rows; REV3 stays the main system; the naming weakness for a silent
  sensor is a stated limitation.
